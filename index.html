New City, New Habits

Every semester, City University asks exchange students to share what has surprised them most since arriving. This semester, we spoke to three students.

A) Lena, 22, Germany

The thing that surprised me most is that almost all the shops close on Sundays, even the small ones near my apartment. At home, some shops are open, so I never had to plan my shopping in advance. Now I always buy everything I need for the weekend on Saturday afternoon, or I have nothing to eat on Sunday! I also notice that Sunday here is really a family day — streets are quiet and most people stay home or visit relatives.

B) Diego, 24, Argentina

For me, the biggest surprise was how quiet public transport is. On the bus or the train, nobody talks loudly, and most passengers are reading a book or listening to something with headphones. Back home, buses are full of conversation and sometimes even music. At first I felt a bit lonely, but now I actually enjoy the calm — it gives me time to think or study before class.

C) Priya, 21, India

I didn't expect how far in advance people plan things here. My classmates book a table at a popular restaurant two or three weeks before going, even for a casual dinner! Where I'm from, you can usually just walk in. Now I try to plan ahead too, but I still miss being able to decide something fun at the last minute.

1Public transport is unusually quiet, with passengers reading or using headphones.
2People usually book restaurant tables far in advance, even for casual dinners.
3Sunday shopping requires planning ahead because most shops are closed.

Learning to surf

Last winter, I decided to try something completely new: surfing. I had been watching surfing videos online for months, and I finally saved enough money for a two-week trip to a surf camp in Portugal.

Before the trip, I bought a wetsuit and read every beginner's guide I could find. I was so excited that I could barely sleep the night before I left. When I arrived at the camp, the owner welcomed me with a big smile and showed me to a comfortable room with a view of the ocean.

The next morning, my problems started. The waves were much bigger than I expected, and the beach was already full of experienced surfers. I couldn't even stand up on the board. Other surfers were laughing and joking around me while I kept falling into the water. By the end of the morning, I felt embarrassed and a little sad.

That afternoon, while I was resting on the sand, an older surfer named Manuel came over and offered to give me a private lesson at a quieter beach nearby. We spent two hours there, and by sunset, I finally stood up on the board for a few seconds. It was a wonderful feeling.

The next day, I went back to the quiet beach with Manuel and two other beginners. We practiced together, helped each other, and laughed a lot. Now, when I think about my surfing trip, I don't only remember the waves — I remember the people I met and how they helped me.

1. Why did the writer decide to try surfing? 1 pt
2. What special item did the writer need for the ocean? 1 pt
3. Describe the writer's mood the night before the trip began. 1 pt
4. What was the beach like on the first morning? 1 pt
5. Who helped the writer improve at surfing, and how? 1 pt
6. Why was the writer's trip memorable? Give your opinion. 1 pt
